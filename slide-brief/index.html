Improving the format of client deliverables may be the most impactful way lawyers can improve the client experience

  1. 1

    Lawyers have spent decades improving the substance and speed of written work.

  2. 2

    The format clients receive it in — email and long documents — has barely changed.

  3. 3

    AI made the reading pile bigger, not clearer.

A memo usually starts the explanation — the call finishes it

  1. 1

    The memo lands. The client reads it.

  2. 2

    Questions pile up: what does this mean for us? What do we do next?

  3. 3

    The lawyer explains it live on a call. That call was the real deliverable.

What changed: an agent can build the explanation environment in minutes

  1. 1

    Before: interactive artifacts took coding skill, design skill, and uneconomic lawyer time.

  2. 2

    Now: the lawyer supplies the work, the audience, and the point to communicate.

  3. 3

    The lawyer stays responsible for the analysis and the final word.

The live build: research memo in, decision map out

Six things make these artifacts work

  1. 1

    Lead with the answer, not the research chronology.

  2. 2

    Build from a small set of tested parts, not invented ones.

  3. 3

    Keep the surface readable; open detail on demand.

  4. 4

    Every proposition can show its source, highlighted, in place.

  5. 5

    The lawyer can change anything before it ships.

  6. 6

    Branding and tone flex; fidelity and evidence do not.

The predictable ways these artifacts fail

  1. 1

    Built right: one decision, one point, sources in reach.

  2. 2

    Breaks it: the whole memo in boxes, undigested.

  3. 3

    Breaks it: decoration that competes with the point.

  4. 4

    Breaks it: citations without provenance or context.
