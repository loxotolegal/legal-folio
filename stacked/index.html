Delaware corporations · officer exculpation explainer

QUESTION · DELAWARE CORPORATIONS

Authorities accessed Aug 17, 2026

Can a Delaware corporation shield its officers from personal liability?

Yes—since Aug. 1, 2022. The 2022 amendment to DGCL §102(b)(7) lets the certificate of incorporation eliminate or limit covered officers’ personal liability for monetary damages for duty-of-care breaches. The charter must contain the provision; the statute did not give officers automatic protection.

Which claims and officers can it cover?

A charter provision can reach direct stockholder claims seeking monetary damages from an officer for a duty-of-care breach. It limits that remedy; it does not erase the duty. 8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)

“Officer” is limited to the president, CEO, COO, CFO, chief legal officer, controller, treasurer, or chief accounting officer; an executive identified as one of the most highly compensated in SEC filings; or a person who agreed in writing to be treated as an officer for this purpose. 8 Del. C. §102(b)(7); 10 Del. C. §3114(b)

How does the corporation adopt it?

First, the board adopts a resolution that sets out the amendment and declares it advisable. The proposal then goes to the stockholders for the required vote. After approval, the corporation executes and files a certificate of amendment. 8 Del. C. §242(b)(1)

The amendment is effective when filed unless it states a permitted later effective time. 8 Del. C. §103(d)

The board must act first; the corporation cannot begin with a stockholder vote.

What can it never cover?

The statute always leaves officers exposed for:

breaches of the duty of loyalty; acts or omissions not in good faith; intentional misconduct; knowing violations of law; and transactions from which the officer received an improper personal benefit. 8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)(i), (ii), (iv)

Unlike directors, officers also remain exposed in actions brought by the corporation or in its right. That means derivative claims stay live against officers. 8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)(v)

The provision also cannot reach an act or omission that occurred before it became effective.

Why would a board do it?

It can put officers closer to directors on protection from monetary duty-of-care claims, reducing a gap that can complicate executive recruiting and retention.

It also changes settlement leverage: a care claim dismissed against exculpated directors could otherwise continue against officers on the same facts.

The amendment narrows one damages remedy. It does not protect loyalty breaches, bad faith, improper benefits, or derivative claims against officers.

This explainer addresses a Delaware stock corporation adding officer exculpation to its certificate of incorporation. It is not legal advice. It reflects the Delaware Code accessed August 17, 2026 and does not analyze a particular charter, class-vote requirement, claim, or officer.

Covered · 8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)

What a covered suit alleges

A stockholder sues the officer directly for money, claiming a decision was careless — that the officer acted without enough information or attention and so breached the duty of care. With the charter provision in place, the officer does not pay personal monetary damages on that claim. The duty itself remains; the shield removes only the damages remedy.

Never covered · §102(b)(7)(i), (iv)

What a disloyalty suit alleges

The claim is that the officer put personal interest ahead of the corporation — self-dealing, taking a corporate opportunity, or receiving an improper personal benefit. That alleges a breach of the duty of loyalty, and the statute does not let a charter shield it.

Never covered · §102(b)(7)(ii)

What a bad-faith suit alleges

The claim is that the officer acted in bad faith — intentional misconduct, a knowing violation of law, or acts or omissions not in good faith. Carelessness can be shielded; knowing wrongdoing cannot.

Never covered · §102(b)(7)(v)

What a corporate or derivative suit alleges

Here the corporation is the plaintiff — directly, or through a stockholder suing on its behalf in a derivative claim. The provision blocks only direct stockholder claims for money; claims by or in the right of the corporation stay live against officers.

8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)

What an officer-exculpation provision may—and may not—do

The certificate may eliminate or limit a covered officer’s personal liability to the corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for fiduciary breach. The text excludes loyalty breaches, bad faith, intentional misconduct, knowing law violations, improper personal benefit, claims by or in the right of the corporation against an officer, and conduct predating the provision.

The 2022 amendment that added officers

Section 1 amended §102(b)(7) to add officers while preserving officer-specific exclusions, including claims by or in the right of the corporation. Section 21 made that change effective August 1, 2022.

Section 21: “Sections 1 through 8, Section 10, Sections 12 through 14, and Sections 16 through 20 shall be effective on August 1, 2022.”

8 Del. C. §242(b)(1)

The board resolution, stockholder vote, and certificate

For a stock corporation, the board first adopts a resolution setting out the amendment and declaring it advisable, then calls a stockholder meeting. After the required stockholder approval, a certificate of amendment is executed and filed.

A certificate setting forth the amendment and certifying that such amendment has been duly adopted… shall be executed, acknowledged and filed and shall become effective in accordance with § 103 of this title.

When the filed amendment becomes effective

A filed instrument is effective on its filing date unless it specifies a later effective time. That later time cannot be more than 90 days after filing.
